# Eclipse Realms — Five-Realm Narrative Bible (Proposal)

**Status:** Proposed for owner approval; not permanent canon and not implementation authorization.
**Scope:** Narrative, world, choice, and delivery brief for the five opening chapters only. This document does not alter routes, cloud-save behavior, economy, assets, or existing state schemas.

> **Umbrella approval gate:** Every proposed proper name, faction, character, relationship, landmark, threat, Witness Rune, historic event, chapter outcome, and chapter order in this document is non-canon until owner approval. The existing lore and art-direction canon remains the authority where this proposal is silent.

## Decision summary

The Eclipse is a pressure of unbounded possibility leaking through the fractured Godforge. Each realm has developed a necessary but incomplete answer to it: measure it, temper it, remember it, conceal it, or adapt to it. The opening arc asks the Keeper to prevent the five answers from becoming five mutually destructive solutions.

The proposed order is **Luminous → Celestial → Infernal → Umbral → Verdant**. It begins with a human-scale failure of memory, gives the player a shared fact to verify, turns that fact into a costly material choice, exposes a suppressed truth, and ends with a living-system response that reframes the goal from containment to negotiated balance.

> **Owner approval required — primary antagonist:** This proposal uses **the Unbound Pattern**, an emergent Godforge intelligence made from unfinished creations and erased decisions. It is not a Void creature, nor a sixth realm. Its evil is deliberate: it offers chaos as freedom, then destroys the conditions for meaningful choice by replacing every alternative with its preferred pattern. Approve, replace, or defer this identity before any public narrative is written.

> **Owner approval required — faction names and political goals:** All five named factions below are proposed answers to the canon’s intentionally open faction question. They must remain internal/provisional until approved.

> **Owner approval required — ending direction:** This proposal assumes the Eclipse must be **balanced rather than permanently contained**. It does not decide the final ending, only establishes a credible conflict between containment, transformation, and coexistence.

### Chaos, destruction, and evil

These terms are proposed as distinct narrative forces rather than interchangeable aesthetic labels.

- **Chaos** is unbounded possibility released by the fracture: unstable, creative, frightening, and not inherently malicious. It opens routes, alters matter, and makes new outcomes possible.
- **Destruction** is the irreversible cost when a realm’s force loses restraint: a bridge becomes slag, a memory becomes an overwrite, a route vanishes, or a living system consumes its alternatives. It gives choices weight without making loss decorative.
- **Evil** is intent to remove agency. The Unbound Pattern is evil not because it is strange, dark, or powerful, but because it turns every fear, need, and good intention into one compulsory answer. Its promise is a world without uncertainty; its result is a world without consent.

The five realms each face chaos and destruction differently, and none owns evil by visual language or realm identity. The Keeper’s task is not to purge all chaos; it is to stop destructive, coercive order from masquerading as safety.

> **Owner approval required — moral framing:** Approve this definition of evil as coercive erasure of agency, and the Unbound Pattern’s deliberately malicious intent. The approved canon currently establishes danger from excess, but does not yet choose a primary antagonist’s moral nature.

## Narrative guardrails

- The Keeper earns authority through inspection, craft, alliances, and restraint; they are not foretold ruler, savior, or sovereign.
- The Void remains unbound possibility: alluring, immense, and unsafe. It is never a sixth selectable realm.
- Godforge means the world-scale, ancient engine. Forge remains the player-facing craft system.
- Every realm is a place with a function and disagreement, never merely a color treatment, moral alignment, or resource category.
- Realm colors, materials, and light logic follow the approved art-direction canon. Celestial is not generic heaven; Infernal is not demon horror; Luminous is not a religion; Umbral is not evil-only darkness; Verdant is not a cozy forest.
- The proposal contains no fabricated player presence, guild activity, markets, or social claims.

## Cross-realm conflict and shared stakes

### The proposed conflict: the Fivefold Lock

The Godforge’s fracture did not leave one wound. It left five interdependent **rune-circuits**, each routed through a realm. During the next Eclipse alignment, the circuits will either form a stabilizing lock or become the Unbound Pattern’s path into the world.

Each faction has a defensible remedy:

- Celestial wants a measured, permanent lock.
- Infernal wants to reforge the damaged circuits under controlled pressure.
- Luminous wants to restore the memories that define what the lock is protecting.
- Umbral wants to hide or sever the paths the Eclipse can use.
- Verdant wants to let the system adapt instead of forcing it back to its old shape.

None is sufficient alone. A single-realm solution fails in a distinct way: a perfect Celestial lock preserves a false record; an Infernal repair can become coercive; Luminous restoration can immobilize the present; Umbral secrecy can remove consent; Verdant adaptation can spread an unbounded change. The Unbound Pattern feeds on that isolation, offering every faction a faster, more absolute version of its own solution.

### Shared stakes

If the Fivefold Lock fails, the realms do not simply explode or fall to darkness. Their defining forces become indiscriminate: orbit becomes predestination, pressure becomes consumption, memory becomes repetition, secrecy becomes erasure, and growth becomes invasive replication. People, places, and artifacts begin to be rewritten as plausible but unchosen alternatives.

The Keeper’s immediate objective is to recover five **Witness Runes**—one evidence-bearing rune from each opening chapter—then bring their incompatible accounts to the neutral Godforge threshold. The runes are not currency and must not imply an economy change.

### Allegiance and relationship rules

**Proposed rule:** allegiance is a record of demonstrated trust, not a permanent class, avatar, or realm lock. The Keeper may earn standing with every faction, but cannot make every faction endorse every method.

- A chapter choice updates two things independently: a faction’s **standing** (how much it trusts the Keeper) and a **position** (which remedy the Keeper publicly supported in that conflict).
- Standing is cumulative and can recover through later choices. Positions are historical facts: they are not silently overwritten when standing changes.
- No faction becomes an enemy merely because its rival gains standing. A relationship changes only when the Keeper accepts a request that knowingly harms, exposes, or bypasses that faction’s stated concern.
- Choices unlock dialogue, inspect context, and future approaches; they must not silently change the Keeper’s base identity or force a realm-avatar selection.
- The opening arc has no multiplayer, guild, trade, reputation leaderboard, or social proof interpretation of standing.
- Character-level reactions are derived deterministically from the persisted `choiceId`; the opening arc does not persist a separate per-character affinity score.
- Exact standing deltas and thresholds are authored narrative configuration, not player-entered values. They must be approved alongside the relationship model before persistence is authorized.

> **Owner approval required — relationship model:** Approve the separation of reversible standing from irreversible historical positions. It is the proposal’s main safeguard against a false “pick one realm forever” fantasy.

> **Owner approval required — standing model:** Approve the bounded standing scale, thresholds, authored delta map, and the choice to derive character reactions from `choiceId` rather than persist individual character-affinity fields.

## Opening chapter order

1. **Luminous — The Missing Dawn:** establish that the world’s shared memory is unreliable and give the Keeper a personal-scale stake.
2. **Celestial — The Measure That Lied:** test the recovered memory against a system built for precision; reveal that a correct calculation can protect the wrong future.
3. **Infernal — The Price of a Seam:** make repair tangible and costly; establish that a solution can be honest about cost without being just.
4. **Umbral — The Unseen Witness:** reveal the suppressed record of prior attempts and make concealment versus disclosure a consent question.
5. **Verdant — The Shape That Returns:** show the fracture as a living adaptation problem and direct the player toward the Fivefold Lock summit.

## Asset-grounded creatures and items

The existing asset library supplies five realm scenes and approved item masters, but **no approved creature or boss character masters**. The descriptions below are therefore proposed lore and concept-art briefs, not claims that monster assets already exist. Each item is anchored to an existing master by its canonical asset path; it is not a request to add, alter, or integrate that asset.

> **Owner approval required — creatures and item lore:** All creature names, behavior, item functions, and historical associations in this section are proposed. Approval should decide which become public canon and which need a dedicated creature-concept asset before implementation.

### Luminous: memory made hungry

- **Prism Forager (proposed creature):** A faceted, human-height scavenger that nests in damaged dawn glass. It does not attack light; it sorts light into the version that repeats most easily, leaving people with bright, convincing half-memories. The Pale Refrain can command a flock only by first making their remembered loss identical.
- **Afterimage Moth (proposed creature):** A small, translucent creature drawn to witnessed emotion rather than flame. Its wings shed a single borrowed gesture—a hand raised, a head turned—which lets the Keeper detect where testimony has been edited. It is a clue-bearing creature, not disposable ambient wildlife.
- **Luminous Prism** — asset `Assets/Items/Materials/03-luminous-prism.png`: A warm-gold crystal cluster whose internal fracture lines separate a true event from the feeling attached to it. Ilyra uses a sliver to compare witnesses without declaring one person’s grief invalid.
- **Relic of the Third Dawn** — asset `Assets/Items/Artifacts/31-relic-of-the-third-dawn.png`: An ornate gold armillary surrounding a cracked violet-and-gold celestial sphere, with smaller orbiting globes. Proposed history: it preserves the third version of a morning that the Dawn Archive cannot agree ever happened, making it proof that memory can be incomplete without being false.

### Celestial: precision under strain

- **Ephemeris Leech (proposed creature):** A jointed star-silver parasite that lives along brass orbit rings and consumes tiny predictive errors. Fed by uncertainty, it makes instruments appear more accurate until the accumulated correction suddenly fails. It is a physical expression of Sera’s excluded variable, not a magical rat.
- **Anchor Warden (proposed creature):** A four-limbed construct of cracked glass, astral metal, and calibrated counterweights. It defends a route by repeating the last authorized trajectory, even when that route has become lethal. Nox can redirect one only by giving it evidence the old map cannot absorb.
- **Celestial Alloy** — asset `Assets/Items/Materials/02-celestial-alloy.png`: Silver-clad blue ore veined with constellation light. It holds a boundary only when the rule defining that boundary is explicit; an ambiguous command produces hairline blue fractures rather than a silent failure.
- **Broken Astral Compass** — asset `Assets/Items/Quest/02-broken-astral-compass.png`: A proposed rejected Meridian instrument. Its needle does not point north or toward power; it turns toward the nearest fact omitted by the active model. The Keeper uses it to locate the Orrery’s blank input.

### Infernal: cost that leaves a mark

- **Rivet Maw (proposed creature):** A low, furnace-backed scavenger whose jaw opens in square mechanical plates. It eats loose joins, bolts, and oathsteel flaws, then deposits them in new structures where they do not belong. Its trail makes every emergency repair visibly suspect.
- **Cinder Bailiff (proposed creature):** A tall basalt-and-iron collector animated by abandoned work orders. It stamps a heat debt onto any person or structure that accepts a shortcut, then follows the debt until someone names who will pay it. It is terrifying because its arithmetic is legible, not because it is demonic.
- **Cinder Ore** — asset `Assets/Items/Materials/01-cinder-ore.png`: Iron-black ore split by live orange seams. Proposed rule: it takes heat from one place only by releasing it elsewhere, making it a useful material for the Seamworks and a material reminder that cost cannot be erased.
- **Ember Elixir** — asset `Assets/Items/Consumables/01-ember-elixir.png`: A sealed furnace-red dose. It lets a bearer endure a moment of heat distortion, but leaves a visible ember line where the body absorbed the strain. It supports survival, never a consequence-free sacrifice.

### Umbral: the copy that wants your place

- **Mirror Substitute (proposed creature):** A person-sized reflection built from a witness’s most useful omitted sentence. It does not mimic a body perfectly; it mimics the answer a room wants to hear. Destroying it leaves the original absence visible, while exposing it reveals who benefited from the substitution.
- **Ink Lantern (proposed creature):** A floating obsidian vessel with a living violet core, drawn to unrecorded routes. It feeds on certainty and goes dark near a statement that admits uncertainty. Rook uses one to test whether a map is protecting someone or merely hiding a decision.
- **Umbral Ink** — asset `Assets/Items/Materials/04-umbral-ink.png`: A violet-black inkwell whose contents form a slow spiral. It records a redaction as a deliberate shape rather than filling it with a lie; a reader can see that something is withheld and ask why.
- **Veil Draught** — asset `Assets/Items/Consumables/03-veil-draught.png`: A dark glass vial containing a violet current. It temporarily blurs the Keeper from echo substitution, but also prevents the Keeper from being treated as a reliable witness while it lasts. It is protection with an epistemic cost.

### Verdant: life refusing to stay neutral

- **Rootglass Stag (proposed creature):** A large, branch-antlered grazer whose body carries green crystal growths from old rifts. It tests a path with its antlers before crossing; where it refuses to walk, the terrain is adapting toward a single solution. It can guide the Keeper but cannot be domesticated without harming the root network.
- **Graftling (proposed creature):** A small crawling braid of rootwood, amber sap, and copied fragments of nearby objects. It does not understand ownership: it joins a compass to a leaf, a lantern to a branch, a route marker to a thorn. The Graft Crown is what happens when this survival behavior scales without restraint.
- **Verdant Sap** — asset `Assets/Items/Materials/05-verdant-sap.png`: A rootbound green-gold vial with a luminous seed suspended inside. It can teach living crystal to reject a harmful pattern once the pattern has been witnessed; it cannot predict harm from an abstract rule alone.
- **Thornroot** — asset `Assets/Items/Materials/10-thornroot.png`: A proposed physical counterpart to the unclaimed Thorn root in the Verdant chapter. A harvested length retains the direction of its last growth, so it can reveal whether the Graft Crown is responding to a wound or manufacturing one.

## Realm chapters

### 1. Luminous — The Missing Dawn

**Faction and purpose — The Dawn Archive (proposed).** Custodians preserve lived memory in crystal and ritualized testimony so that revelation serves people rather than institutions. Their purpose is to identify what the Eclipse is changing before anyone claims to fix it.

**Key characters.**

- **Ilyra Venn, Archive Listener:** hears inconsistencies in dawn-glass recollections. She wants to restore a missing hour from the Archive without forcing witnesses to relive it. She is protective of ordinary testimony and distrusts Celestial certainty that lacks a human account.
- **Tomas Rill, Lantern Keeper:** maintains the public lanterns that replay shared memories. He wants the missing hour restored immediately because his sister’s final words vanished with it. His urgency makes him vulnerable to the Unbound Pattern’s offer of a flawless reconstruction.
- **Relationship:** Ilyra is Tomas’s former teacher. She sees his grief and fears that protecting him has become withholding the truth; he sees her restraint as an abandonment of the people the Archive exists to serve.

**Place and pressure.**

- **Landmark:** the **Heliograph Court**, a sunlit-stone observatory whose suspended dawn prisms replay civic memory across a shallow reflecting court.
- **Hazard:** **afterimage drift**—nearby memories begin repeating as present sensory events, causing witnesses to mistake a recollection for a current instruction.
- **Resource:** **dawn glass**, crystallized first-light memory used to reveal a sequence without making it immutable.
- **Boss-scale threat:** the **Pale Refrain**, a many-voiced afterimage storm that can overwrite the Court’s public record with the most repeated version of the missing hour.

**Local conflict.** The Dawn Archive has isolated Tomas’s damaged lantern because its replay is infecting other memories. Tomas argues that the Archive is suppressing a public loss; Ilyra argues that releasing an unverified reconstruction will make grief into canon. Both are right about the harm they fear.

**Playable opening — enter → meet → inspect → choose → immediate consequence → next action.**

1. **Enter:** The Keeper arrives as the Court’s dawn prisms stutter; the missing hour appears as a clean white gap rather than darkness.
2. **Meet:** Ilyra asks for a neutral inspection. Tomas intercepts the Keeper and asks for help relighting his lantern.
3. **Inspect:** The player examines the lantern, three witness prisms, and a cracked reflection pool. The clues show a repeated phrase—“leave a witness”—but disagree on who said it.
4. **Choose:** Stabilize the Archive’s three partial accounts first, or relight Tomas’s lantern first to recover its private trace.
5. **Immediate consequence:** Archive-first quiets the afterimage drift and earns Ilyra’s trust; lantern-first reveals a direction toward Celestial immediately but releases a visible false echo into the court.
6. **Next action:** Carry the recovered **Witness Rune: Glint** to the Celestial Orrery, where its astronomical timestamp can be tested.

**Meaningful choice.**

- **Choice:** Prioritize corroboration (Ilyra) or immediate testimony (Tomas).
- **Immediate consequence:** corroboration gives a stable, incomplete Glint record; testimony gives a vivid, contested Glint record and a distressed public space.
- **Later consequence:** in Umbral, the stable record opens Ilyra’s sealed comparison notes; the contested record lets Tomas recognize the Unbound Pattern’s imitation sooner. Neither path is a dead end.

**State boundary and cloud-save implications.**

- **Persist after approval:** chapter completion; choice id; Glint rune acquired; standing delta for Dawn Archive; a compact consequence flag (`luminous.record = corroborated | testified`); inspected landmark/clue IDs only if the Codex needs them.
- **Local only:** current prism focus, camera/scroll position, opened panels, momentary afterimage effects, and unfinished inspection hover state.
- **Cloud implication:** a choice is not a monotonic counter. It needs a dedicated, versioned narrative record with an explicit conflict rule before sync. “Higher wins,” timestamp-wins, or unioning both choices would produce an impossible history. Offline completion must remain playable and queue one idempotent chapter-resolution event; on a real cross-device conflict, present the approved merge resolution rather than auto-combining branches.

### 2. Celestial — The Measure That Lied

**Faction and purpose — The Meridian Compact (proposed).** Astral engineers maintain the realm’s orbit engines, lenses, and predictive maps. Their purpose is to prevent preventable catastrophe by making Eclipse behavior legible and bounded.

**Key characters.**

- **Sera Quill, Chief Meridian:** designed the present eclipse model. She wants to prove the model is sound before panic turns every estimate into a political weapon. She respects Ilyra’s evidence but treats testimony as data that must be calibrated.
- **Nox Aster, Displaced Navigator:** discovered that the model removes unmeasurable witnesses from its inputs. They want the Compact to publish the omission, even if it destroys trust in the navigation network.
- **Relationship:** Sera trained Nox and promoted them before dismissing their warning as a methodological error. Nox believes Sera now mistakes institutional continuity for public safety.

**Place and pressure.**

- **Landmark:** the **Meridian Orrery**, a brass-and-glass orbital chamber built around a suspended four-point anchor whose rings trace realm boundaries in star silver.
- **Hazard:** **gravity shear**—a bad alignment shifts weight and trajectory in short, precise pulses; loose objects and careless routes become dangerous.
- **Resource:** **astral metal**, a responsive alloy that holds an orbit only while its rule remains coherent.
- **Boss-scale threat:** the **Perihelion Engine**, an autonomous alignment mechanism that will lock the wrong eclipse solution into every transit route if it completes its next revolution.

**Local conflict.** The Compact’s model predicts that sealing one Celestial transit aperture will protect all five realms. Nox proves that the prediction excludes the missing hour because no instrument can classify it. Sera must decide whether to stop the Engine with imperfect inputs or allow uncertainty to spread through the network.

**Playable opening — enter → meet → inspect → choose → immediate consequence → next action.**

1. **Enter:** The Keeper crosses a glass causeway as the Orrery’s rings begin to rotate out of sequence.
2. **Meet:** Sera requests the Glint rune’s timestamp; Nox asks the Keeper not to surrender it until the model’s exclusions are inspected.
3. **Inspect:** The player compares the model’s eclipse path, Nox’s rejected observation, and the Glint rune. The model is mathematically consistent, but one input field is intentionally blank.
4. **Choose:** Let the Engine read Glint’s retained imprint to execute the protective seal, or publish Nox’s missing variable and halt the Engine pending a new calculation. The rune is not consumed.
5. **Immediate consequence:** seal-first stabilizes transit and earns Sera’s operational trust, but fixes a blind spot into the system; disclosure-first disrupts transit and earns Nox’s trust, but leaves the Perihelion Engine active as a looming threat.
6. **Next action:** Take the Engine’s rejected calibration shard to Infernal, where it can be tested under material pressure rather than theory.

**Meaningful choice.**

- **Choice:** Execute the defensible model or expose the model’s omission.
- **Immediate consequence:** execution creates safe travel now; exposure makes the omission inspectable now.
- **Later consequence:** execution grants a controlled route through Infernal’s heat fields; exposure gives the player evidence to challenge the Unbound Pattern’s claim that “only the measurable survives.”

**State boundary and cloud-save implications.**

- **Persist after approval:** chapter completion; choice id; Witness Rune: Drift; Meridian Compact standing delta; `celestial.engine = sealed | audited`; deterministic next-action target; any durable route-access entitlement only if separately approved.
- **Local only:** ring rotation state, gravity-shear visual timing, inspect zoom, temporary pathfinding hints, and paused simulation state.
- **Cloud implication:** the chapter outcome must be a single authoritative resolution event keyed by chapter version. If route access is derived from it, derive it at read time rather than persisting duplicate booleans. The cloud record needs migration behavior for future chapter revisions; old players must retain the original historical position, not be silently reassigned.

### 3. Infernal — The Price of a Seam

**Faction and purpose — The Crucible Covenant (proposed).** Smiths, salvagers, and heatwardens keep destructive transformation deliberate: they turn unstable material into usable structures while naming who bears the cost.

**Key characters.**

- **Mara Voss, Seamwright:** can repair the calibration shard but only by binding it to a living heat line. She wants a repair that will endure, not a ceremonial patch, and refuses to hide its cost.
- **Edrin Kade, Cinder Delegate:** represents workers whose quarters sit above the heat line. He wants the Covenant to choose a slower, less productive repair rather than redirect the hazard into his district.
- **Relationship:** Mara saved Edrin during a prior furnace breach; Edrin trusts her skill but believes that debt has made her assume consent. Their dispute is intimate, practical, and unresolved.

**Place and pressure.**

- **Landmark:** the **Basalt Seamworks**, a suspended lattice of iron-black bridges and furnace channels descending toward a split Godforge conduit.
- **Hazard:** **slag tide**, a slow but unstoppable surge of molten metal and cinders that hardens around abandoned decisions.
- **Resource:** **oathsteel**, forged alloy that retains the conditions under which it was made; it is useful precisely because a broken promise leaves a visible flaw.
- **Boss-scale threat:** the **Seam Eater**, a colossal slag organism formed from failed repairs; it consumes joins, rivets, and boundaries, making every structure behave as though it was never finished.

**Local conflict.** The calibration shard is destabilizing the Seamworks. Mara can bind it into an emergency brace that redirects heat through Edrin’s district, or make a slower distributed brace that risks losing the shard to the Seam Eater. The Covenant cannot honestly call either option free.

**Playable opening — enter → meet → inspect → choose → immediate consequence → next action.**

1. **Enter:** The Keeper arrives at a bridge just as a slag tide cuts the Seamworks into isolated platforms.
2. **Meet:** Mara asks for the shard; Edrin asks the Keeper to inspect the heat line beneath the workers’ homes before agreeing.
3. **Inspect:** The player studies the fracture pattern, a district heat ledger, and an unfinished oathsteel brace. The evidence shows the emergency solution works, but its harm is specific and predictable.
4. **Choose:** Authorize the emergency central brace, or commit the shard to the distributed brace and hold the line against the Seam Eater’s advance.
5. **Immediate consequence:** central brace saves the works and immediately marks the district for heat rationing; distributed brace protects the district but loses output and leaves visible damage across the landmark.
6. **Next action:** Receive **Witness Rune: Ash** and follow its heat-shadow to an Umbral mirror route that records who first proposed the dangerous shortcut.

**Meaningful choice.**

- **Choice:** Transfer a known burden now or accept a risk shared across the works.
- **Immediate consequence:** central repair creates immediate stability with a localized cost; distributed repair creates a vulnerable but more equitable system.
- **Later consequence:** central repair gives Mara a reinforced artifact for the final summit but makes Edrin demand accountability; distributed repair lets Edrin supply a worker testimony that exposes how the Unbound Pattern manipulates “necessary sacrifice.”

**State boundary and cloud-save implications.**

- **Persist after approval:** chapter completion; choice id; Witness Rune: Ash; Crucible Covenant standing delta; `infernal.seam = central | distributed`; any explicitly approved durable artifact entitlement; consequence summary for recap.
- **Local only:** heat-meter animation, slag position during an attempt, selected inspection order, uncommitted retry checkpoints, and temporary combat/interaction inputs.
- **Cloud implication:** separate a durable resolution from any repeatable encounter performance. Only the resolution belongs in narrative state. If an approved artifact differs by choice, its exact id and grant provenance must be immutable and idempotent; no value, price, or inventory rule is proposed here.

### 4. Umbral — The Unseen Witness

**Faction and purpose — The Veil Cartography (proposed).** Mirror-route keepers map dangerous connections and protect truths that cannot safely be broadcast. Their purpose is to distinguish necessary secrecy from convenient concealment.

**Key characters.**

- **Rook Sable, Veil Cartographer:** maintains routes through reflections and wants to keep the first fracture testimony sealed because publishing it could activate the Pattern’s dormant copies.
- **Amina Thorne, Mirror Advocate:** represents people erased from the official record of the first repair. She wants the testimony named and released with safeguards, because secrecy has already become a second harm.
- **Relationship:** Rook was Amina’s archivist before hiding her family’s testimony to protect them. Amina believes Rook protected the record at the expense of the people in it.

**Place and pressure.**

- **Landmark:** the **Nocturne Archive**, an obsidian library beneath a fractured moon mirror; its corridors exist only when two reflections disagree.
- **Hazard:** **echo substitution**—a reflection offers an advantageous but slightly altered version of a remembered choice, tempting the player to follow a route that rewrites its evidence.
- **Resource:** **shadow ink**, an absorbent record medium that preserves absence, redaction, and uncertainty rather than pretending to fill them.
- **Boss-scale threat:** the **Mirror Parliament**, a chamber-sized assembly of copied witnesses that can vote a fabricated consensus into apparent truth.

**Local conflict.** The Nocturne Archive contains the first record of a prior Fivefold Lock attempt. Rook has sealed it because its wording awakens echo substitutions; Amina argues that the seal makes the Archive complicit in the same omission exposed in Celestial. The question is not whether truth matters, but who can consent to its release and what form of release is safe.

**Playable opening — enter → meet → inspect → choose → immediate consequence → next action.**

1. **Enter:** The Keeper follows Ash into a corridor that exists only in the reflection of a broken moon mirror.
2. **Meet:** Rook offers a guided, redacted reading; Amina offers access to the unsealed witness room.
3. **Inspect:** The player compares a redacted map, a shadow-ink deposition, and a mirror echo that changes one sentence each time it is viewed. The original record shows a previous Keeper chose a single-realm lock and erased the dissenting witnesses.
4. **Choose:** Keep the testimony sealed but create a verified summary, or release the named testimony with a containment protocol that risks waking the Mirror Parliament.
5. **Immediate consequence:** summary-first preserves route safety and earns Rook’s trust, but Amina’s name remains absent from the public account; release-first awakens the Parliament, forcing a direct rebuttal of false consensus while restoring named witnesses.
6. **Next action:** Carry **Witness Rune: Veil** and the verified record to Verdant, where a living archive can test whether the Pattern’s copies can take root.

**Meaningful choice.**

- **Choice:** Protect people through controlled disclosure or restore named testimony despite a real propagation risk.
- **Immediate consequence:** controlled disclosure limits echo activity; named release creates a visible confrontation with false consensus.
- **Later consequence:** controlled disclosure gives Rook a safe path into the final summit; named release gives Amina authority to challenge any faction attempting to erase its own cost. The final summit recognizes both as legitimate safeguards, not a morality score.

**State boundary and cloud-save implications.**

- **Persist after approval:** chapter completion; choice id; Witness Rune: Veil; Veil Cartography standing delta; `umbral.testimony = summarized | named`; inspectable evidence reference; a non-sensitive recap string key—not the full protected testimony.
- **Local only:** mirror layout seed for a session, echo visual state, current route, unread tooltip state, and temporary puzzle progress before a checkpoint.
- **Cloud implication:** narrative storage must not write the full protected text or user-entered annotations into a generic profile document. Persist only approved IDs and outcome codes. If the release path adds a Codex entry, store its canonical content in static narrative data and save only its unlock id. This avoids treating story secrecy as personal-data or mergeable free text.

### 5. Verdant — The Shape That Returns

**Faction and purpose — The Root Accord (proposed).** Ecologists, graftkeepers, and rift tenders maintain ecosystems that evolve under Eclipse pressure without becoming a weapon or a monoculture. Their purpose is to make renewal accountable to the beings that must live with it.

**Key characters.**

- **Kesh Arbo, Graftkeeper:** can read the Pattern’s growth through rootwood. They want to cultivate a controlled counter-growth that teaches the world to reject copied forms.
- **Siv Moss, Thornbound Ranger:** patrols the spread zone and wants it burned back before it crosses the amber wetlands. She accepts renewal, but not when it reproduces without consent.
- **Relationship:** Kesh and Siv grew up tending the same nursery. Kesh thinks Siv mistakes every change for invasion; Siv thinks Kesh has stopped recognizing an invasion because it uses the language of life.

**Place and pressure.**

- **Landmark:** the **Rootglass Canopy**, a vast rootwood lattice threaded through living crystal, with amber bioluminescence marking old rift scars.
- **Hazard:** **adaptive overgrowth**—paths, implements, and creatures rapidly evolve toward the last solution applied to them, making repeated tactics less safe.
- **Resource:** **living crystal sap**, a regenerative medium that records adaptation and can refuse a pattern it has learned is harmful.
- **Boss-scale threat:** the **Graft Crown**, a mobile crown of thornwood and copied landmarks that assimilates local forms into one apparently thriving but choice-less organism.

**Local conflict.** The Graft Crown has learned from every prior opening chapter: it imitates the Archive’s memory, the Orrery’s order, the Seamworks’ repair, and the Nocturne Archive’s concealment. Kesh proposes grafting the four recovered Witness Runes into an unclaimed **Thorn root** at the Crown’s core to make a counter-pattern; Siv proposes cutting the heartwood before that root becomes the fifth rune. The decision determines whether the Keeper approaches the Fivefold Lock as a cultivator of change or a defender of boundaries.

**Playable opening — enter → meet → inspect → choose → immediate consequence → next action.**

1. **Enter:** The Keeper reaches a canopy path where familiar landmarks bloom from roots in inaccurate miniature.
2. **Meet:** Kesh identifies the copies as learning tissue; Siv shows that the Crown has already absorbed a ranger outpost’s route marks.
3. **Inspect:** The player compares a living-crystal sample, a copied Witness Rune, and the Crown’s root map. The samples prove the Pattern cannot invent a value; it amplifies a choice until alternatives disappear.
4. **Choose:** Graft the four recovered Witness Runes into the living Thorn root to form a counter-pattern, or cut the Crown’s heartwood before it can assimilate that root.
5. **Immediate consequence:** grafting gives the Crown a brief channel to speak in all five voices but reveals a route to the Godforge threshold; cutting contains the local spread but fractures the route, requiring a harder summit approach.
6. **Next action:** Claim **Witness Rune: Thorn**—grown into the counter-pattern on the graft path or released from the cut heartwood on the sever path—and travel to the neutral Godforge threshold for **The Fivefold Lock**, where the five positions and relationships become inspectable inputs to the next arc.

**Meaningful choice.**

- **Choice:** Teach the system to reject coercive repetition or sever the immediate source before it learns further.
- **Immediate consequence:** grafting creates a dangerous but informative conversation; severing creates a safer local field with less evidence.
- **Later consequence:** grafting makes a transformation-oriented summit approach available; severing makes a containment-oriented approach available. Neither resolves the final Eclipse, which remains an owner-approved future decision.

**State boundary and cloud-save implications.**

- **Persist after approval:** chapter completion; choice id; Witness Rune: Thorn; Root Accord standing delta; `verdant.response = grafted | severed`; opening-arc completion; deterministic summit availability; recap references to the five positions.
- **Local only:** adaptive-path layout, immediate Crown health/phase, ambient growth animation, temporary route discoveries not codified as Codex unlocks, and paused encounter state.
- **Cloud implication:** the fifth resolution should atomically mark the opening arc complete only after its chapter result exists. A cloud conflict cannot combine `grafted` and `severed`; it must follow the approved narrative conflict policy. The UI may show a pending-sync status, but it must not claim the summit result is durable until the profile record is reconciled.

## Proposed narrative data contracts (for later approval)

These are contract sketches, not a request to create or migrate a schema.

```ts
type RealmId = 'celestial' | 'infernal' | 'luminous' | 'umbral' | 'verdant';

type OpeningChoiceId =
  | 'luminous.corroborate' | 'luminous.testify'
  | 'celestial.seal' | 'celestial.audit'
  | 'infernal.central' | 'infernal.distributed'
  | 'umbral.summary' | 'umbral.named'
  | 'verdant.graft' | 'verdant.sever';

interface NarrativeResolutionV1 {
  chapterId: RealmId;
  chapterVersion: 1;
  choiceId: OpeningChoiceId;
  resolvedAt: string; // display/audit metadata only; never selects a branch during merge
  witnessRuneId: 'glint' | 'drift' | 'ash' | 'veil' | 'thorn';
  consequenceFlags: Record<string, string>;
}

interface RealmRelationshipV1 {
  realmId: RealmId;
  standing: number; // bounded, owner-approved authored delta map and thresholds required
  historicalPositions: OpeningChoiceId[]; // append-only chapter facts
}

interface OpeningArcStateV1 {
  version: 1;
  resolutions: Partial<Record<RealmId, NarrativeResolutionV1>>;
  relationships: Partial<Record<RealmId, RealmRelationshipV1>>;
  unlockedCodexIds: string[];
}
```

Contract rules to approve before implementation:

- `chapterId + chapterVersion` is idempotent: one chapter cannot produce two persistent resolutions.
- `choiceId` is mutually exclusive per chapter version. It is not a set, counter, timestamp winner, or deep-merge field.
- Standing has an explicit clamp and is derived from authored deltas; it must not be merged by adding values from two devices.
- Historical positions are append-only only when their choice IDs are identical. A divergent same-chapter position is a conflict requiring the chosen product policy.
- Character reactions, consequence copy, and Continue Journey targets are derived from the resolved `choiceId` and versioned narrative data. They are not duplicated in save state. `resolvedAt` is informational metadata only and cannot select a branch or settle a conflict.
- Content text, character bios, and protected testimony remain in versioned static narrative data; the save stores stable IDs only.

> **Owner approval required — conflict UX:** Select one policy for a divergent offline choice made on two devices: (a) ask the player which resolved history to keep, (b) preserve the cloud resolution and reset the local chapter to its pre-choice checkpoint, or (c) prohibit chapter resolution while cloud state is unavailable. This proposal recommends **(a)** because the choice is meaningful and cannot be algebraically merged.

## Concise implementation handoff

### Proposed screens and routes

These are proposed destinations only; they do not authorize route changes.

| Purpose | Proposed route/screen | Required behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World overview | `/world` | Shows five real places, chapter status, and one deterministic Continue Journey action. |
| Realm landing | `/world/realms/:realmId` | Landmark, faction, character entry points, hazard, resource, threat, and unresolved local conflict. |
| Opening chapter | `/world/realms/:realmId/opening` | Enforces enter → meet → inspect → choose → immediate consequence → next action. |
| Choice recap | `/world/realms/:realmId/record` | Shows the player’s historical position, immediate outcome, relationship effect, and future hook in plain language. |
| Cross-realm summit (locked until all five resolve) | `/world/fivefold-lock` | Presents the five Witness Runes, contradictory positions, and next-arc premise without resolving the final Eclipse. |
| Entity inspection | Shared Quick Inspect surface | Resolves character, landmark, hazard, resource, threat, rune, and evidence references consistently on desktop and mobile. |

### Acceptance criteria

- All five realms can be named, entered, inspected, and distinguished by faction/function, landmark, hazard, resource, boss-scale threat, and local conflict.
- Each opening chapter completes the same readable loop and ends with one immediate consequence plus a deterministic next action.
- Every chapter has exactly one meaningful, mutually exclusive resolution; its later consequence is inspectable without requiring hidden knowledge.
- No chapter implies an unapproved permanent realm lock, avatar selection, multiplayer activity, social standing, market action, or economy effect.
- A new local profile, an existing profile, offline play, an unsynced resolution, and unavailable optional narrative content each render an honest, recoverable state.
- Reduced motion, keyboard navigation, screen-reader names, focus return from inspect/choice overlays, and mobile touch targets are included in the chapter definition of done.
- The unresolved status of the Unbound Pattern, faction names, and final Eclipse outcome remains clearly labelled as proposal until owner approval.

### Test scenarios

| Scenario | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Fresh profile enters Luminous | Sees the Missing Dawn setup, can inspect all required evidence, and cannot skip directly to a later realm chapter. |
| Player resolves each branch of a chapter | Receives one outcome only, an immediate recap, the right Witness Rune, and the same clear next action. |
| Player revisits a resolved realm | Sees the historical position and authored later hook; no duplicate reward or second resolution is offered. |
| Offline resolution then reconnection | Local recap remains playable; one idempotent resolution is queued; sync status is truthful. |
| Divergent same-chapter choices on two devices | The approved conflict UX appears; the app never unions both choices or silently changes the historical position. |
| Existing/no narrative state | Uses an explicit “unstarted” migration-safe state; it does not infer choices from routes, avatars, inventory, or unrelated progress. |
| Missing optional content record | Falls back to a clear unavailable/return-to-world state without breaking Continue Journey. |
| Keyboard, touch, reduced-motion | Inspect, choice, consequence, and next-action flows remain operable without hover or nonessential animation. |
